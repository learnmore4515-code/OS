The Operational Challenge

Insurance brokers in Kenya run on paperwork — KYC documents, claim forms and onboarding paperwork must be collected from clients before a policy can be bound or a claim paid. Today that chasing is manual: WhatsApp threads, SMS, email and spreadsheets held in separate places, with no single source of truth.

The workflow’s purpose. Document chasing automation: a broker opens a case, the system sends the client a secure, tokenised upload link on their preferred channel, tracks every response, and flags anything still open after seven days to an exception queue for senior review. The broker decides; the system nudges, tracks and escalates.

Why manual chasing fails

These failure modes come from discovery conversations with brokers (Deliverable C of the project), not assumptions:

  • Understanding Clients don’t understand what is needed — requests are incomplete or confusing.
  • Submission Documents are difficult to submit — no mobile-friendly upload path, no app installation acceptable.
  • Trust Clients distrust upload links and prefer WhatsApp or email as an alternative.
  • Tracking Brokers lose track of who owes what; follow-ups get forgotten across disconnected channels.
  • Parties Client, broker, insurer and loss adjuster all share responsibility, with no shared status view.
  • Escalation Cases overdue more than 7 days have no structured path to a senior broker.

No revenue figures, conversion rates or client counts are claimed here — this is a capability demonstration built on the project’s own research, not a delivered production deployment.

Scope note. Document chasing only — payment reconciliation, automated underwriting and policy issuance are explicitly out of scope (Deliverable F).
