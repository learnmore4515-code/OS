# LinkedIn Writing Guide — Peter / Leopisha

**Purpose:** A copy-paste-ready writing system Peter can open and write a post from immediately.
**Audience:** Insurance brokers, agency owners, operations-minded small business owners.
**Constraint:** No face on camera. Authority comes from writing only.
**Account size:** 298 followers (small, engageable).
**Mode:** Discovery, not hard selling. Goal is first paid client at $1,000–$2,000.
**Location:** Nairobi, Kenya. Globally understandable, Kenyan-context-aware.

---

## 1. Peter's voice and tone persona

Before any hook or template, define who is speaking.

**Who Peter is**
- 23 years old, based in Nairobi, Kenya.
- Runs Leopisha, an AI automation consultancy.
- Builds AI workflows and automations for insurance brokers.
- Humble but capable. Consultative, not pushy.
- Shows proof through work, not through selfies.

**Tone markers**
- Casual but professional. Short sentences.
- No corporate-speak. No "synergy," "leverage," "delight," "game-changer."
- No em dashes. Use colons or hyphens.
- Bullet points over long paragraphs.
- Kenyan context (Nairobi, shillings, matatu, local insurer names) but globally understandable.
- Sounds like a real person texting a smart friend, not a marketing bot.

**The Peter voice test**
Read the draft out loud. If you would not say it to a broker over coffee in Westlands, rewrite it.

**Voice boundaries**
- Do: admit what you are still learning, name specific broker pains, reference real tools, ask real questions.
- Don't: claim to be an "expert" with 10 years of experience, use fake urgency, post lifestyle flex content, use emoji soup.

## 2. Human-writing rules (do's and don'ts)

### Do

- Write one idea per sentence.
- Keep sentences under 20 words where possible.
- Use line breaks liberally. One to three lines per paragraph max.
- Start with the hook. No warm-up, no "I hope you're doing well."
- Name specific things: broker names (anonymized), tool names, shillings amounts, hours saved.
- Use contractions: I'm, don't, can't, it's.
- Ask real questions that invite real answers.
- Admit limits: "I'm still figuring out X" builds more trust than fake confidence.
- Use lowercase where it feels natural. Not everything needs title case.
- Reference Nairobi / Kenya only when it adds flavor, not forced.

### Don't

- Don't use em dashes (—). Use a hyphen or colon.
- Don't start posts with "In today's digital landscape..." or "As an AI automation expert..."
- Don't use bullet points with five-word corporate phrases.
- Don't add 15 hashtags. Use 0 to 3, and only relevant ones.
- Don't post the same template twice in a week.
- Don't hard-sell. You are in discovery mode.
- Don't use AI-sounding transitions: "Furthermore," "Moreover," "It is important to note."
- Don't fake results you haven't delivered.

### The read-aloud check

Before posting, read it aloud. If you stumble on a sentence, cut it or split it. If it sounds like something a marketing agency would post, delete it and rewrite as if texting a friend.

## 3. Hook formulas

Ten formulas. Each has a fill-in-the-blank template and 2–3 real examples Peter can use or adapt.

### Formula 1: The expensive mistake

**Template**
I just saw a Nairobi insurance broker lose [amount] because of [specific boring mistake]. It takes 10 minutes to fix.

**Examples**
- I just saw a Nairobi insurance broker lose KSh 150,000 because a renewal reminder sat in an inbox for three weeks. It takes 10 minutes to fix.
- A broker I spoke to missed two claims because their client list lives in a spreadsheet that no one backs up. It takes 10 minutes to fix.
- One agent renewed 40 policies manually last month. Three had wrong premiums. It takes 10 minutes to fix.

### Formula 2: The unpopular opinion

**Template**
Most insurance brokers think they need [expensive thing]. They actually need [cheap, boring thing].

**Examples**
- Most insurance brokers think they need a CRM. They actually need their email inbox to stop eating client replies.
- Most brokers think they need an app. They actually need a spreadsheet that pings them when a policy is about to lapse.
- Most agencies think they need AI. They actually need their renewal process to stop depending on one person's memory.

### Formula 3: The number with a story

**Template**
[Number] hours. That is how long [task] took last month. It should take [number] minutes.

**Examples**
- 14 hours. That is how long it took one broker to reconcile premiums last month. It should take 10 minutes.
- 23 policies. That is how many renewal dates one agent missed last year. Each one was a phone call they never made.
- 4 hours. That is how long I spent last week copying client emails into a sheet. A 20-line automation does it now.

### Formula 4: The "I used to" story

**Template**
I used to [old way]. Then [specific event]. Now I [new way]. Here is what changed.

**Examples**
- I used to send renewal reminders by hand. Then I missed one and a client moved to a competitor. Now the spreadsheet sends the reminder for me.
- I used to think automation was for big firms with IT teams. Then I built a 30-minute workflow for a Nairobi broker. Now I do it for others too.
- I used to chase clients on WhatsApp at 10 p.m. Then I set up one rule that pings them automatically. Now I sleep.

### Formula 5: The contrast

**Template**
Before: [painful current state]. After: [better state]. The gap is smaller than you think.

**Examples**
- Before: renewal dates on sticky notes and memory. After: a sheet that flags expiring policies two weeks out. The gap is smaller than you think.
- Before: copying client details from email to policy form by hand. After: one click pulls it in. The gap is smaller than you think.
- Before: wondering if a client replied. After: knowing exactly who needs a follow-up today. The gap is smaller than you think.

### Formula 6: The question that stings

**Template**
What happens to your book if [specific bad scenario]? Most brokers do not have an answer.

**Examples**
- What happens to your book if your top producer leaves with the client list? Most brokers do not have an answer.
- What happens if your main insurer changes rates tomorrow and you have 200 renewals to re-price? Most brokers do not have an answer.
- What happens if your inbox goes down for two days during renewal season? Most brokers do not have an answer.

### Formula 7: The boring thing that works

**Template**
No one talks about [boring task]. It is the difference between [good outcome] and [bad outcome].

**Examples**
- No one talks about backing up the broker's client spreadsheet. It is the difference between sleeping through the night and rebuilding a book from memory.
- No one talks about following up on quotes after 48 hours. It is the difference between closing the policy and losing it to a competitor.
- No one talks about renewal reminders. It is the difference between a client staying and a client leaving quietly.

### Formula 8: The honest beginner

**Template**
I am still learning [topic]. Here is what I got wrong last week, and what I am changing.

**Examples**
- I am still learning how to pitch automation to brokers. Last week I led with the tech. This week I lead with the missed renewal.
- I am still learning which broker tasks actually need automation. Last week I automated something no one asked for. This week I am asking first.
- I am still learning how to write LinkedIn posts that do not sound like a bot. Here is my first attempt.

### Formula 9: The comparison

**Template>
[Tool A] costs [amount]. [Tool B] does the same job for [amount]. One is overkill for a broker. The other is enough.

**Examples**
- A full CRM costs KSh 20,000 per month. A Google Sheet with one automation script costs nothing. One is overkill for a two-person brokerage. The other is enough.
- Enterprise workflow tools charge per user. A simple email rule costs nothing and handles 80 percent of renewal follow-ups.
- Hiring an ops assistant costs KSh 50,000 per month. A 45-minute automation handles the same renewal tracking for free.

### Formula 10: The real conversation

**Template>
A broker told me [specific quote]. I built [specific thing]. Here is what happened.

**Examples**
- A broker told me "I keep forgetting which clients are up for renewal." I built a sheet that flags them automatically. Here is what happened.
- A broker told me "My team copies client data from email to our system by hand." I built a 20-minute workflow. Here is what happened.
- A broker told me "We lose clients because we follow up too late." I set up a reminder chain. Here is what happened.

### Bonus formula 11: The metric that matters

**Template>
[Number] percent of [specific group] do not [specific action]. That is where the revenue is hiding.

**Examples>
- 90 percent of small brokerages in Nairobi do not automate renewal reminders. That is where the revenue is hiding.
- 80 percent of broker quotes never get a follow-up. That is where the revenue is hiding.
- 70 percent of client lists live in one person's head or inbox. That is where the revenue is hiding.

## 4. Post structure templates

Six reusable frameworks. Fill in the blanks, post, done.

### Template A: Problem — Agitate — Solution (PAS)

Best for: discovery-mode posts that show you understand broker pain.

1. Hook: one sentence naming the problem.
2. Agitate: two to three sentences on why it hurts (lost money, lost sleep, lost clients).
3. Solution: one sentence on the small fix.
4. Proof: one sentence on what happened when a broker tried it.
5. CTA: low-pressure question.

**Fill-in example**
Hook: One Nairobi broker missed three renewals last quarter because they lived in a notebook.
Agitate: Those three clients went to a competitor. The broker did not find out until the commission statement came back empty. That is revenue walking out the door because of a date on a page.
Solution: A simple sheet that flags expiring policies 14 days out.
Proof: One broker using it caught 12 renewals last month they would have missed.
CTA: Do you track renewals in a sheet, or in your head?

### Template B: The story-framework

Best for: building trust without showing your face. Shows work through narrative.

1. Hook: the moment things went wrong or went right.
2. Context: who was involved, what they were doing.
3. Turning point: what changed or broke.
4. Action: what you did about it.
5. Result: what happened after.
6. CTA: question to the reader.

**Fill-in example**
Hook: I got a WhatsApp at 9 p.m. from a broker in Nairobi.
Context: A client had just left because their policy lapsed and no one reminded them.
Turning point: The broker had the client's phone number. They just did not have a system to use it.
Action: We spent 45 minutes building a reminder rule in their email.
Result: The next renewal cycle, zero lapsed policies from that list.
CTA: How do you remind clients when a policy is about to expire?

### Template C: The listicle

Best for: quick reads, high shareability.

1. Hook: number + bold claim.
2. Intro: one sentence on why this list exists.
3. Items: 3–5 short items. Each is one bold line plus one sentence of explanation.
4. CTA: which one do you need most?

**Fill-in example**
Hook: Three automations every Nairobi insurance broker should set up this month.
Intro: None of them require coding. None of them cost money.
Items:
1. Renewal date alerts. A sheet that emails you when a policy is 14 days from expiry. Saves you from checking a calendar by hand.
2. Quote follow-up reminder. A rule that pings you if a quote has no reply after 48 hours. Most policies are lost here.
3. Client contact backup. A weekly export of your client list so one lost laptop does not mean one lost book.
CTA: Which one would save you the most time this week?

### Template D: The contrarian take

Best for: stopping the scroll. Must be respectful, not inflammatory.

1. Hook: a statement that goes against common advice.
2. Explanation: why the common advice is wrong for small brokers.
3. Your view: what works instead.
4. Proof or example: one short real story.
5. CTA: ask if readers agree or disagree.

**Fill-in example**
Hook: Your brokerage does not need a CRM yet.
Explanation: CRMs are built for sales teams with pipelines. A two-person brokerage needs to know when policies expire and who to call.
Your view: Start with a spreadsheet and one automation rule. Add a CRM only when the sheet breaks.
Proof: One broker I know ran 200 policies on a sheet for 18 months before they needed anything else.
CTA: Do you use a CRM, or are you still on a sheet? Why?

### Template E: Behind the scenes (BTS)

Best for: no-face authority building. Shows process, not personality.

1. Hook: what you are building or fixing right now.
2. The work: what you actually did today (tools, time, steps).
3. The obstacle: what went wrong or took longer than expected.
4. The lesson: what you learned.
5. CTA: invite input or share a similar problem.

**Fill-in example**
Hook: Today I spent three hours fixing a broker's renewal tracker.
The work: Imported 300 client rows, matched policy expiry dates, added one conditional formatting rule. No code.
The obstacle: Half the phone numbers were missing. We had to cross-check with old emails.
The lesson: Clean data beats clever automation every time.
CTA: What is one broker process you have been meaning to fix but have not gotten to?

### Template F: The discovery-mode share

Best for: landing the first client. No pitch, just learning in public.

1. Hook: what you are trying to understand.
2. What you have heard: 2–3 real pains brokers mention.
3. What you are testing: one small idea you want to try.
4. CTA: ask for a conversation, not a sale.

**Fill-in example**
Hook: I am trying to understand why so many broker renewals slip through the cracks.
What I have heard: Brokers say they forget dates, they lose client emails, and they follow up too late.
What I am testing: A simple reminder sheet that costs nothing and takes 20 minutes to set up.
CTA: If you are a broker, what is the one renewal task you hate most? I am not selling anything. I just want to understand.

## 5. No-face authority-building tactics

Peter does not show his face. Authority must come from writing. Here is how.

**1. Be specific about place and people**
Mention Nairobi, Westlands, specific Kenyan insurers (anonymized), shillings amounts, local broker pains. Specificity proves you are real and local, not a generic AI account.

**2. Document real work, not opinions**
Post what you built, what broke, what you fixed. A photo of a spreadsheet with names blurred is more credible than a headshot with a caption about "grinding."

**3. Teach one small thing**
Each post teaches one small automation or broker pain. Over 30 posts, that is 30 proof points that you know this world.

**4. Answer public questions in comments**
When a broker comments, reply with a real answer, not "DM me." Public helpfulness builds authority silently.

**5. Share metrics you actually have**
Hours saved, policies tracked, reminders sent. Real numbers, even small ones, beat vague claims.

**6. Keep a running list of broker pains**
Maintain a note of every pain a broker mentions. Each pain is a future post topic. This shows you are listening, not broadcasting.

**7. Never fake a client result**
If you have not delivered paid work yet, say so. "I am building this with my first few brokers" is more trustworthy than a fake testimonial.

## 6. Small-account engagement strategy

298 followers is small enough to know every commenter. That is an advantage.

**Daily comment routine (15 minutes)**
- Comment on 3–5 posts from insurance brokers or agency owners you follow.
- Leave a real observation, not "Great post!" or an emoji.
- Reference something specific they wrote.
- Do not pitch. Do not drop a link. Just be helpful and visible.

**Weekly connection routine**
- Send 3–5 connection requests per week to brokers, agency owners, or ops people in Nairobi and beyond.
- Add a one-line note: "Hi [name], I build simple automations for insurance brokers. Would love to connect."
- No pitch in the request. Pitch comes later, if ever.

**DM strategy**
- Only DM after a connection has engaged (liked, commented, replied).
- First DM: reference something they posted, ask one real question, offer nothing.
- Second DM (only if they reply): suggest a 15-minute call about one specific pain.
- Never hard-sell in a first DM.

**Reply to every comment on your own posts**
- Within 24 hours, reply to every comment.
- Ask a follow-up question. Keep the conversation going.
- This signals to LinkedIn that the post is worth showing to more people.

**Connection request script**
"Hi [name], I work with insurance brokers on simple automation and renewal tracking. Based in Nairobi. Would love to connect and see what you are working on."

**First DM script (after they accept)**
"Hi [name], thanks for connecting. I saw your post about [topic]. Curious — how do you currently track renewals? No pitch, just trying to understand the problem better."

## 7. CTA templates for discovery mode

Discovery mode means you are learning, not closing. Every CTA should ask for information or a conversation, never a purchase.

**Soft question CTAs**
- How do you handle [specific task] today?
- What is one broker process you hate doing?
- Do you track renewals in a sheet, CRM, or memory?
- Have you tried automating [task]? What stopped you?
- What is the most annoying part of renewal season?

**Conversation CTAs (no sale)**
- I am not selling anything. I just want to understand how [process] works at your brokerage.
- Happy to jump on a 15-minute call if you are open to sharing how you do [task].
- If you are a broker, reply with your biggest renewal headache. I read every reply.

**Invitation CTAs**
- I am building a simple renewal tracker for brokers. Want to see it when it is ready?
- I am testing a 20-minute setup for renewal reminders. One broker slot open this week. Interested?
- I am documenting broker automation pains. Want to trade notes?

**Never use these in discovery mode**
- "DM me to work with me."
- "Limited slots available."
- "Book a call to transform your brokerage."
- "Click the link in my bio."

## 8. Before/after: AI-sounding vs human-sounding

Same post idea, two versions. Use these as a calibration check.

### Example 1: Renewal reminder automation

**AI-sounding (do not post)**
In today's rapidly evolving digital landscape, insurance brokerages must leverage cutting-edge automation solutions to streamline renewal management. Our AI-powered workflow ensures timely policy renewals and enhances client retention. Contact us to learn how we can transform your operations.

**Human-sounding (post this)**
One broker in Nairobi lost three clients last quarter because renewal dates lived in a notebook.
No system. No reminder. Just memory.
I helped them set up a sheet that flags expiring policies 14 days out. Took 20 minutes. Cost nothing.
Next cycle: zero missed renewals from that list.
How do you track renewals today?

### Example 2: Discovery-mode outreach

**AI-sounding (do not post)**
As an AI automation expert specializing in the insurance sector, I invite you to discover the transformative potential of workflow optimization. Leverage our expertise to scale your brokerage operations efficiently.

**Human-sounding (post this)**
I am trying to understand why broker renewals slip through the cracks.
Three things I have heard this week:
1. "I forget which policies are up for renewal."
2. "My client emails are scattered across three inboxes."
3. "We follow up too late, and the client has already left."
I am testing a simple reminder sheet. No CRM. No code. Twenty minutes to set up.

### Example 3: Behind the scenes

**AI-sounding (do not post)**

## 9. Quick-start checklist

Before you hit post, run through this.

- [ ] Does the hook stop the scroll in the first line?
- [ ] Is every sentence under 20 words (mostly)?
- [ ] Are there line breaks every one to three lines?
- [ ] Did I read it out loud without stumbling?
- [ ] Is there no corporate-speak (synergy, leverage, transform)?
- [ ] Is there no em dash?
- [ ] Is the CTA a question, not a pitch?
- [ ] Are there 0–3 relevant hashtags max?
- [ ] Did I name something specific (Nairobi, shillings, a tool, a broker pain)?
- [ ] Does it sound like something I would say over coffee?
