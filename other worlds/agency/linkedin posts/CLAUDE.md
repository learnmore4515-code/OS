## 3. Content system

### Four pillars (all drawn from real broker pains in `responses.md`)

**P1 — The renewal trap.** Renewal follow-ups and notices (Benson's 2-month / 1-month / 1-week / 3-day sequence), chasing cedants and reinsurers (Brenda), renewal-season submission chasing (Martin). One post = one pain + one way automation removes it.

**P2 — The document chase.** Signed documents (Josephine), exposure data and claims experience (Martin), motor documents — logbook, ID, KRA PIN (Kelvin), material-facts disclosure (Kelvin). Show a tracker that removes the chase.

**P3 — Policy admin and data entry.** Asset additions/deletions, client data collation, insurer amendments (Darlington). Show data entry removed.

**P4 — Build in public (no face).** What Leopisha is building, stack walkthroughs (Botpress, Stack AI, n8n), pricing decisions, mistakes, demo videos (screen only). CTA: book a 10-minute discovery call.

### No-face formats

- **Image post (default):** professional image + copy, one funnel stage, under 300 words of copy. Repurpose the same image + copy across Top / Middle / Bottom instead of writing from scratch.
- **Carousel:** 5-7 slides, problem → current manual cost → automated flow → result → CTA.
- **Screen-recorded demo:** 60-90 seconds, Botpress or n8n running, voice-over optional, no face — only where it directly proves the method.
- **Poll:** "Which task eats most of your brokerage week?" Options: renewal follow-ups, chasing documents, policy admin, other.
- **Testimonial / proof image:** client quote + blurred name + payment proof screenshot (amounts blurred if needed).

### Hook rules

- Lead with broker pain / time / money / focus, never with "AI" or founder identity.
- Hook must land in the first 150 characters / 2–3 lines before "see more".
- No income claims.
- One idea per post.
- CTA is optional. If used, it must serve a clear purpose (e.g., book a discovery call). Never append a comment-keyword CTA just to drive engagement.
- No founder-first framing ("I am 23, Nairobi, no face..."). Lead with broker pain, not the founder's bio.

### Writing standards (non-negotiable)

Every post must pass this checklist before publishing:

- **Anchor to real broker pain.** Every post must trace to a specific broker conversation in `responses.md`. Name the broker (or role), the specific task, and the time/money cost. No generic "automation helps brokers" posts.
- **No manufactured credibility.** Peter has zero paid clients and zero delivered automations. Do not imply templates, systems, results, or case studies he hasn't earned. Say "I'm building" not "I built."
- **Specific insight over broad take.** Show a technical decision, trade-off, mistake, surprising observation, or concrete example from the actual n8n/Botpress workflows Peter is developing. Do not publish generic "automation removes the chase" advice that any freelancer could write.
- **No engagement bait.** Never append "Comment X if..." or similar engagement-bait CTAs. A post can end with a strong observation, a useful conclusion, or a genuinely necessary question.
- **No unverified claims.** Distinguish between what Peter has researched (research/), what he has built, what he has tested, and what has worked in production. When evidence is missing, do not fill the gap with invented details.

### Quality check (before publishing)

Reject the post if any answer is yes:

- Could hundreds of automation freelancers publish substantially the same post?
- Is the central insight not specific to Peter's research, work, or experience?
- Does any factual claim lack support in `responses.md` or `research/`?
- Is the hook interesting because of manufactured controversy rather than the idea itself?
- Does the post pretend Peter has results he does not have?
- Does the ending feel engineered for comments rather than natural?

### Funnel rhythm: TPMGT (Top → Middle → Bottom → Top)

Every post sits in one funnel stage. Never pitch two posts in a row — always follow a Bottom (conversion) post with a Top (reach) post. Only Top builds reach; only Bottom converts; the rhythm is what does both.

- **T — Top of funnel (reach):** relatable founder story, festive or high-value take, something slightly contrarian. One idea, no pitch. Goal: reach and new followers. Examples: "Your brokerage does not need a CRM yet"; "23, no face, 298 followers — building Leopisha in public."
- **M — Middle of funnel (methodology / authority):** how Leopisha actually works — the renewal reminder sequence (2-month / 1-month / 1-week / 3-day), the document tracker, policy admin automation — step by step, exactly what we do and how. Filter: proves we have something real.
- **B — Bottom of funnel (conversion):** case studies, play wins, exactly how to work with us — with a CTA at the end and a link in the post (link goes in the first comment per LinkedIn reach rules). Pitch post. Examples: "$1,000 case-study rate, one broker this November"; client result post.
- **T — Top again:** repurposed professional image post + copy to refill reach before the next conversion post.

Correct pillar-to-funnel mapping:
- T (Top/reach): P4 build in public + contrarian takes + founder stories. Goal: reach and new followers. Never pitch.
- M (Middle/methodology): P1/P2/P3 how-to. One pain + one automation removes it. Filter: proves we have something real.
- B (Bottom/convert): testimonials, case studies, direct offer + CTA. Pitch post. Link in first comment only.
- T (Top again): repurposed professional image post + copy. Refill reach before the next conversion post.

### Weekly cadence (TPMGT rhythm)

- Phase 1: 3 posts/week — Tuesday Top (reach, contrarian / founder story), Wednesday Middle (methodology / how-to, no pitch), Thursday Bottom (conversion, offer + CTA). Post at audience-local time: Tuesday 10 AM, Wednesday 4 PM, Thursday 12 PM.
- Phase 2: 4 posts/week — add Friday Top (repurposed image post).
- Phase 4: 5 posts/week — add Monday carousel (checklist, Top).
