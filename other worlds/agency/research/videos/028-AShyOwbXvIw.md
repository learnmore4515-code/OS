## What this video is actually about
A beginner-first-client playbook for an AI automation service: find local service businesses without websites on Google Maps (example: a Charleston hair salon/day spa with ~50 reviews and no website), build them a completely free website using HighLevel sub-accounts and Canva (templates, ~10 minutes each), show it in a free meeting with no selling, then upsell paid AI services on top — appointment calendar (~$50-$75/mo), Google-review AI ($75/mo), website widget ($75/mo), and a voice receptionist — stacking to ~$150/mo per client. The creator frames the free website as a "decoy offer" that creates reciprocity so people feel compelled to buy upsells. He supports it with personal claims: now making $110,000/month, it took him 4 months to land his first client, and a car-detailer client paid him $10,500 over 2.5 years at ~$50 payment increments.

## Key lessons
- The first client / first payment is what makes an online business real; most people quit before landing it (they expect results in the first 5 hours and give up).
- Give before you ask: a free website decoy offer builds reciprocity and credibility before any paid ask.
- Don't custom-code ("we are not living in 2005"); use HighLevel templates so a site takes ~10 minutes to build.
- Do good work anyway: real testimonials (pull from Google reviews), real address, phone, email, domain the business already owns, Canva logo — so it doesn't look like a template job.
- Call ~20 businesses without websites per day (1-2 hours/day) until you have 5-10 free websites built.
- In the first meeting show only the working website; don't sell anything yet. Say yes to all changes for free.
- Upsell after the free website: calendar/booking sub-account, Google-review AI, website widget, voice receptionist.
- One client can be worth thousands over time (car detailer: $10,500 over 2.5 years).
- This method lands the first client and can get to ~$10K/month; it is not, by itself, a $50-100K/month path (creator's own words).

## Exact business model discussed
Decoy-offer service model targeting local service businesses (hair salons/day spas, car detailers, nail bars) that lack a website.
- Lead gen: Google Maps → businesses with modest review counts (~50) and no website.
- Core free product: a fully built + hosted website at zero cost to the client ("no hidden fees, no monthly cost afterwards. Absolutely nothing.").
- Monetization: paid AI-automation upsells delivered through HighLevel sub-accounts — appointment calendar ($50-$100/mo suggested to start; stated as $75/mo in the pitch), Google-review AI ($75/mo), website widget ($75/mo), voice receptionist (books appointments on missed calls). Stacked example: $0 → $150/mo from one customer.
- Cost structure: HighLevel starter plan is $97/mo and agency-unlimited plan is $297/mo, but the creator says his "1% community" membership covers HighLevel so members can run the method for free with unlimited sub-accounts.

## Customer
Local service businesses without a website, found on Google Maps, ideally with good but relatively few reviews (e.g., 50 reviews, 4.9 rating) versus competitors with far more reviews.
- Worked example: "Salon 120 Day and Spa" (Charleston), ~50 reviews, no website, good reviews.
- Competitors named on the call: Safaria, Max Studio, Chroma (review counts cited as 160 and 385).
- Also referenced: a local car-detailer client held for 2.5 years.
- Businesses where someone misses calls while working (haircut example), making the voice-receptionist upsell relevant.

## Problem
- The business has no website and no online booking ("I couldn't seem to find a way to book online").
- Beginners doing cold outreach get no responses, no callbacks, and quit, concluding online business is a scam or not for them.
- The beginner has no testimonials, no credibility, and feels entitled to clients despite just learning the skill.
- Businesses miss phone calls while working / out of hours / on closed days, losing booking revenue.

## Offer
Free-website decoy offer, scripted verbatim:
"Would you be against me setting up your website completely for free? No hidden fees, no monthly cost afterwards. Absolutely nothing. Completely for free. I'll build it for you. I will show it to you. If you don't like it, then we just split ways and we call it a day. But if you do like it, you get the website completely for free, host it completely for free."
Pitch opener: "Hey, I check you out on Google Maps. I can see you have a bunch of reviews and people love your service, but I couldn't seem to find a way to book online."
Creator's framing: "It's called a decoy offer." / "an offer that's so good that people would feel stupid to say no." Note: he cautions this offer may not be viable once making $10-20K/month (no time); it is a beginner tactic.

## Acquisition
1. Google Maps search for a local niche in a chosen market (demo: "hair salons" in Charleston).
2. Scroll results, pick a business with modest reviews (~50) and no website; get its phone number.
3. Call; if no answer, text the opener script above.
4. Handle the "no website yet / not set up" objection with the decoy-offer close.
5. Volume: call 20 businesses without websites every day; takes 1-2 hours/day; continue until you have 5-10 free websites to build.
6. Book a meeting to show the finished website; show, don't sell.
7. After results, ask for a Trustpilot testimonial; happy clients refer other local shops ("that one customer might become free customers because he tells free friends").

## Sales
- First meeting: show the actual functioning website; do not try to sell anything yet. Goal is to make them say "damn I want this as my website."
- They will ask for changes ("Can we change this and this and this?"); answer "Yes, I can do this completely for free for you" and make all changes free.
- Then upsell: calendar/booking sub-account where they manage appointments ("charge let's say 50 bucks a month for this or 100 bucks a month... You want to start small"; in the pitch he says "it's 50 bucks a month. It's 75 uh bucks a month").
- Next upsell: Google-review AI ($75/mo) — after every appointment it texts the customer; if happy, it sends them a Google review; if not, it doesn't.
- Next upsell: website widget ($75/mo) — answers customer questions based on company info and prices.
- Additional upsell: voice receptionist inside HighLevel — picks up missed calls (out of hours/closed days) and books available times; creator notes "It's not like it's going to take over the calls for you."
- Stacked result cited: "$0 a month for this customer to $150 a month from this customer."
- Close by asking for a testimonial on Trustpilot after they've gotten results.
- Stack the same proven agent across clients ("I just set this up for another client. You want this as well?").

## Fulfillment
1. HighLevel sub-account → Sites → Websites → New website → pick a template (~10 minutes to build).
2. Choose a template fitting the brand; customize thoroughly (don't make a "terrible website").
3. Logo: create in Canva, search niche (e.g., "salon"), pick colors from a screenshot via an image picker/color tool ("hexi from picture"), download PNG with background removed, upload to HighLevel, size ~100px.
4. Copy: read the business's Google reviews and Facebook page; use their language ("enhance the beauty of your hair with our expert care"); include how long they've been in business; if a detail is missing, "write something that makes sense."
5. Images: Canva photos (e.g., haircuts, pedicure) or pictures from the business's Facebook page.
6. Testimonials: insert real Google-review screenshots as testimonials (not fake).
7. Contact page: correct address, phone, email (if findable), domain they already own; insert logo; copyright footer with their name and year.
8. Preview with animations; confirm it looks good; it's a fully functioning website they need.
9. Book a meeting, show the site, make free changes, then launch/host free.
10. Add the calendar to the contact page (HighLevel calendar settings: new "appointment" calendar, 60 minutes, availability 8am-5pm, custom URL like "hair-appointment").
