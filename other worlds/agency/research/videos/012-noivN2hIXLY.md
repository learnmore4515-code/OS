## What this video is actually about
Liam Ottley (founder of Morningside AI) breaks down his agency's exact process for performing a $60,000 AI audit. It is a follow-up to his previous video on whether AI is replacing agencies. Morningside pivoted from pure development into strategic consulting ("AI transformation partner" model) and now uses the AI audit as an entry point / extended sales cycle that leads into development deals. The video covers: Morningside's consulting pivot journey, the $50 billion SMB consulting gap, the $60,000 audit process in detail, a breakdown of one real audit (Asma Steel, New Zealand), and a simplified two-week beginner framework for offering AI audits. A resource pack (interview guides, AI opportunity matrix, ROI calculator, ready-to-use slides) is offered via the first link in the description.

## Key lessons
- Companies are not buying dev work anymore; they are buying clarity in AI confusion, and the dev work comes after. "And the dev work is just what comes after it."
- Tie consulting to delivery: "anything that we recommend, we're the ones who are going to be on the hook for delivering it." This breaks the "consultants are just wafflers" stereotype.
- Deals "kind of close themselves" after you finish an audit because you know everything about the client's business.
- The audit is an extended sales cycle (4-6 weeks for mid-market; 2 weeks for small business) that builds trust so development deals follow easily.
- Validate constantly with the client during the audit ("stay in the loop"); don't surprise them with final recommendations at the end of week four.
- Start with small businesses (5-10K two-week assessment), then move up market as case studies build.
- AI project failure is 80-85% vs ~40% for normal IT projects, creating disillusionment and a demand for expert help.

## Exact business model discussed
AI transformation partner model with a three-phase client journey:
1. Discovery: audits and road mapping (the $60K audit)
2. Actual implementation (development services)
3. Partnership and ongoing optimization / ongoing consulting
The AI audit is the entry point / Trojan horse. Morningside brands itself as an AI transformation partner rather than a pure dev shop. The audit is productized (four-week proven format, ~75-slide presentation) and positioned as a sales cycle that converts into development pipeline. Big consultancies (Deloitte, PWC) only chase 100k-500k deals with large companies, leaving the SMB market untapped.

## Customer
Two distinct categories Ottley calls "small smalls" and "big smalls":
- Small smalls: typically 1 employee up to ~200 (lean operations — digital agencies, info businesses, mom-and-pop service businesses). They want immediate ROI, quick action, "what's the best tools now?" Fast decision-making by the founder.
- Big smalls: ~100-200+ employees (mid-market). Complex decision-making processes, complex and legacy systems (not make.com/Airtable/Go High Level). They want clarity, education on what AI is, culture transformation help, and a "lay of the land" — implementation is not top of mind yet.
Example client: Asma Steel, a steel company in New Zealand (~200-300 employees, mid-market), engaged via a warm connection to Ottley's business partner.

## Problem
- AI encroachment on traditional dev/automation services: Claude, Microsoft Copilot, Cursor, Bold, Lovable, and NAT make it easier for businesses to self-serve AI automation development, commoditizing pure development work.
- Businesses can build AI but don't know where to put it — value moves from development to consulting.
- AI projects fail at 80-85% (vs ~40% for normal IT projects like a custom CRM), leading to disillusionment and companies scrapping AI initiatives.
- Massive talent gaps: companies struggle to hire internal AI expertise and don't know what to hire for.
- Big consultancies ignore the SMB segment (100k-500k deals only); no one is serving small-to-medium businesses with full-service AI strategy + development.
- SMBs have limited budgets (5-10k, maybe 20k) but need help.

## Fulfillment
Four-week audit structure (the "proven format"):
- Week 1 — Discovery and mapping: stakeholder interviews (e.g., 35 calls in one week for one client; small business needs 3-5 key team members, or 10-15 interviews; 30-45 min each, remote or in person). Outputs: interview summary document, process flow diagram, preliminary pain-point inventory. Map current-state processes: acquisition, delivery, support systems; decision trees, key people, value creation processes. Mark bottlenecks with red stars.
- Week 2 — Solution design: build opportunity database from process maps and interviews; technical feasibility assessments (where dev experience matters); difficulty-vs-value impact matrix (quick wins = low difficulty/high value; big swings = high difficulty/high value). Validate with client via calls ("temp checking"). Deliverables: top 3 opportunities, 90-day implementation roadmap, optional 6-month roadmap, executive presentation slide deck.
- Week 3 — Validation: stay in the loop with users/team, float ideas before finalizing, avoid surprising the client at the end.
- Week 4 — Final presentation: 12-month roadmap with quick wins for immediate ROI; estimated ROI and development costs per recommendation; convert to development deals.

## Exact tactics
- Offer your first audit free to warm connections to learn the process (accept the loss as a bet).
- Conduct 30-45 minute stakeholder interviews with 3-5 key people (small business) or 10-15 interviews.
- Build a current-state process map covering acquisition, delivery, and support systems; mark bottlenecks with red stars.
- Use a difficulty-vs-value impact matrix to separate quick wins from big swings.
- Validate recommendations with the client continuously ("temp checking") rather than presenting a surprise at the end.
- Deliver a 12-month roadmap with quick wins for immediate ROI.
- Tie every consulting recommendation to your own delivery capability ("we're on the hook for delivering it").
- Position shift: from "I build AI systems" to "I transform businesses with AI."

## Mistakes / warnings
- First free 8-week audit cost $30,000 loss (intentional bet to learn).
- Don't do short 2-hour discovery calls — not comprehensive enough, no full picture.
- Don't wait until the end of week four to present recommendations; the client will be upset if surprised. Stay in the loop throughout.
- Don't give "pie in the sky" recommendations; base them on what's technically feasible and what the client's team can actually adopt.
- Consultants have a "dirty word" reputation (wafflers); break it by tying consulting to development delivery.
- It's a tough shift; takes a lot of work; 6-8 months to refine the process.
- Small smalls want immediate ROI; big smalls have complex legacy systems and slower decisions.

## What I should NOT conclude from this video
- That $60K audits are easy or guaranteed; Morningside's first audit lost $30,000 and took 6-8 months to refine.
- That you need no development experience — dev experience "really comes into play" for feasibility assessments.
- That big consultancies (Deloitte, PWC) are direct competitors for SMB work — they explicitly ignore the SMB segment.
- That the two-week beginner audit delivers the same depth as the four-to-eight-week version — it is explicitly "a simplified two-week assessment."
- That this works without warm connections/network — the first (Asma Steel) client came via a warm connection to the business partner.

## Relevance to eventual AI-agency playbook
Provides a productized audit offer as an entry point / Trojan horse to development deals; a pricing ladder (free → 5-10K → 30K → 60K → 80K+); a concrete four-week delivery framework (week 1 discovery/interviews/process maps, week 2 solution design/impact matrix/roadmap, week 3 validation, week 4 presentation/12-month roadmap); warm-connection acquisition; and a positioning shift from pure dev to "AI transformation partner" that justifies higher fees and longer client relationships.
