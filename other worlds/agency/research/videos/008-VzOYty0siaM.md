## What this video is actually about
Nate Herk, co-founder of True Horizon AI (an AI consultancy that helps businesses implement AI), explains how he made $231,000 in 30 days as an AI consultant and breaks down the two paths to becoming an AI consultant starting over: Path 1 = B2C (educating the market via YouTube, school communities, courses) and Path 2 = B2B (working directly with businesses — consulting, done-for-you automations, full AI implementation packages). He also gives a 5-step framework and a 30-day action plan for replicating it from scratch.

## Key lessons
- An AI consultant takes knowledge of AI and applies it to a business or individual to save them time or make them money (teaching beginners to build with AI, or systematizing operations for seven-figure business owners).
- Use the doctor approach: diagnose problems before prescribing a solution, rather than the pharmacist approach of handing over a pre-built workflow.
- Two sides of the business: B2C (educational, scalable, one-to-many, higher volume, lower ticket) and B2B (high ticket, low volume, direct consulting/implementation).
- The majority of his $231K in 30 days came from the B2C side because it's more scalable (impacts thousands at once); B2B still has money (consulting firms clear hundreds of millions per year).
- People pay for shortcuts, clarity, and accountability — a faster path to success, not just information.
- B2B brings money faster but less leverage at first; B2C gives leverage but takes longer to build trust and an audience.
- The two paths are a flywheel: B2B client work gives proof/testimonials that fuel B2C content; B2C audience feeds back into the B2B pipeline. Start where you have the most momentum.
- Focus on problems, not tools; use the minimum amount of AI possible; sometimes the best answer is a $30 SaaS or a simple non-AI automation.
- Pick one leverage tool and go deep (for Nate, n8n); depth builds authority.
- Document everything publicly; transparency builds trust; public documentation becomes your portfolio and brings inbound.
- Build simple offers (clear, easy to understand, easy to buy).
- Overdeliver and systematize; every project becomes proof (testimonials, referrals, case studies) and part of your digital resume.

## Exact business model discussed
- B2C (educational side): school communities (main revenue source, combining learning, accountability, and community), YouTube channel, courses, Notion templates, digital courses, one-on-one coaching, cohort programs, paid Discords — teaching beginners, freelancers, and small business owners how to use tools like n8n to implement or sell AI solutions.
- B2B (True Horizon AI consultancy): diagnosing where AI fits into operations, designing implementation roadmaps, helping clients execute; consulting, done-for-you automations, full AI implementation packages; monthly retainers for continuous improvement.
- B2B education/enablement: True Horizon has been paid tens of thousands of dollars just to educate teams (a few hours showing how to build something, how it works, or walking through their AI audit process).
- The $231K came from multiple income streams: school community, consulting projects via True Horizon AI, and implementation work — not one huge client deal or launch.

## Customer
- B2C: beginners, freelancers, and small business owners who want to learn to implement or sell AI solutions (e.g., using n8n).
- B2B: small to medium businesses that can't afford premium rates charged by big firms (McKinsey, BCG, Accenture); businesses with inefficient processes (example given: losing ~50% of inbound leads because they don't follow up quickly).
- The common inbound prospect: someone who says "I know I need to start using AI, but I don't know how. I don't know where to start."

## Problem
- Businesses know they need AI but don't know how or where to start.
- Inefficient operations waste time, money, and focus (e.g., slow speed-to-lead losing half of inbound leads).
- Big consulting firms charge hundreds of thousands of dollars just for AI audits or roadmaps, which small/medium businesses can't afford.
- Need to save time, save money, or scale without hiring more people.

## Offer
- B2C example: "learn how to automate your business using n8n" — delivered via YouTube videos, school communities, Notion templates, digital courses, one-on-one coaching.
- B2B examples: "we'll automate three workflows for your business"; "we'll audit your sales process and show you exactly where AI can save you time and money"; consulting, done-for-you automations, full AI implementation packages; monthly retainers for continuous improvement; AI audits; team education sessions (a few hours teaching teams how to use/build with AI).
- Offer should be clear, easy to understand, and easy to buy.

## Acquisition
- B2C: give away free value through YouTube so people trust you before they ever reach out; inbound demand, no cold DM, no outreach, no hard sell; public documentation (builds, before/after results, failures) acts as a portfolio and brings inbound opportunities.
- B2B: referrals; getting embedded in a single company and expanding horizontally into departments; free AI automation audits as the foot in the door / primary market research.
- Flywheel: B2B client projects → proof, testimonials, confidence → B2C content → B2C audience → feeds back into B2B pipeline.
- No audience or fancy funnels needed for B2B; just solve one business problem really well.

## Sales
- Doctor approach: identify the problem (what's costing the business time, money, or focus), study workflows/SOPs, find inefficiencies, design an AI or automation solution.
- Free audits → some businesses ask "can you just build this for us now?" → first paid project; first few builds are about reps and real-world proof, not profit.
- Overdeliver → testimonials, referrals, case studies → more clients, more content, more credibility.
- When value is clear, clients stay as long-term partners paying monthly retainers for continuous improvement.
- Anchor price off the actual value added; rule of thumb: within a year of implementation, the business owner should see 10x return on the initial investment, making it a no-brainer.
- First client is the hardest; momentum builds through referrals.

## Fulfillment
- Diagnose where AI fits into operations, design implementation roadmaps, help clients execute; build and deliver AI systems for clients.
- Systematize how you onboard clients, deliver, and share results; build systems around idea → test → feedback → improvement; the quicker you fail, the quicker you learn and win.
- Keep the team lean; focus on deep relationships; one high-paying client is preferable to managing many lower-paying clients.
- Overdeliver on first few clients to get testimonials, referrals, and case studies; track before/after results (hours saved, revenue gained, costs reduced) and turn them into case studies and content.

## Exact tactics
- Pick a niche from days 1–3: an industry you understand or have connections in, simple to work with, not heavily regulated, lots of repeatable manual tasks (examples: real estate, coaching, e-commerce, marketing agencies; maybe avoid healthcare or finance).
- Market research days 3–10: study the niche (Reddit threads, LinkedIn posts, use chatbot to brainstorm pain points, talk to people), uncover three clear problems worth solving, learn their language.
- Free AI automation audits days 10–15: reach out offering free audits — not selling, but learning and primary market research; foot in the door and credibility builder.
- Turn free audits into paid clients days 15–20: some businesses ask "can you just build this for us now?" — that's the first paid project; overdeliver (reps, not profit).
- Days 20–30: overdeliver and turn results into case studies; track before/after (hours saved, revenue gained, costs reduced); post results, processes, takeaways as content to attract inbound and build authority.
- Document everything publicly as you go (builds, before/after, failures, what's working).
- Pick one tool and go deep; be known as the person who solves X problem using Y tool.
- Keep offers simple: clear, easy to understand, easy to buy.
- Systematize onboarding, delivery, and results-sharing.

## Mistakes / warnings
- Don't lead with the tech; lead with the problem you're solving.
- Don't overcomplicate the offer.
- Don't wait until you think you're an expert to share; document as you go.
- Don't assume you need a huge following (small following can be an advantage — high-touch experience).
- Don't start with healthcare or finance (heavy regulations).
- Don't build custom AI if a $30 SaaS or simple non-AI automation solves it.
- You don't need to be super technical — just a few steps ahead of the people you're helping.
- B2C is slower to start (time to build trust, following, consistent content); B2B is faster money but less leverage at first.
- The first client is the hardest.
- The $231K figure comes from multiple income streams, not one huge client deal — don't interpret it as a single-deal result.
- "Anyone can build to that point" is the speaker's claim, not a guarantee.
