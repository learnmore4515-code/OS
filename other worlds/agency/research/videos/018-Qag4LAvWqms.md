## Key lessons
- Start narrow: pick one niche/solution type and become an expert in it quickly (you can be an expert "in literally a month if you pick one specific tool"), then broaden over time.

- Solve the three problems (technical expertise, lead flow, credibility/authority) at once by documenting your learning publicly (builds authority + lead flow) and converting interest into free consulting calls (closes deals on the phone).
- Don't go the SAS route unless you have millions/VC; don't jump to consulting/education immediately with no proof.
- The AI agency is a vehicle to learn the space faster than everyone else; it can later set you up for SAS or other models.

## Exact tactics
- Pick ONE of the five narrow solution types as your entry point; do not try all five at once.
- Learn make.com and Airtable if choosing AI automation specialist (no-code, lowest technical barrier).
- For text-based agents: build custom ChatGPT-like experiences via GPTs/Assistants API, deploy on WhatsApp/websites; program at prompt level (no code needed).
- For voice agents: pick one platform (Bland AI, Synth Flow, Vapi) and get good at it; expect code/get "gnarly" if doing custom front-end integrations with Vapi.
- For Zapier ecosystem: use tables, zaps, canvases, chatbots — all no-code; Zapier Central for AI agents.
- For chatbots: use Voiceflow, program/design piece by piece with full logic control; deploy on WhatsApp with buttons.
- Document your learning journey on YouTube/LinkedIn while you learn (do both simultaneously).
- Offer free consulting calls to convert interest into paying clients; close on the phone.
- After 2–3 months, once you have expertise + lead flow + authority, broaden into other areas.

## Mistakes / warnings
- Don't go the SAS route (needs millions of dollars / VC connections — "not accessible to most of us").
- Don't jump straight to consulting or education immediately — you have "no tangible proof of how we've been able to help businesses with AI"; a "LinkedIn AI Guru who's all they've ever done is done Consulting" can't get paid much for that information.
- Don't spend 6 months learning without sharing your journey — you'll be "at Square One when it comes to marketing yourself and getting some lead flow."
- Don't try to do all five agency types at once — pick one narrow entry point.
- Voice agents can get "nerd on the code" (Vapi/custom integrations) and custom front-end interfaces can get "Gilly" (gnarly).
- Text-based agents can be "too wild and unhinged" for some clients who want more control — chatbots may be better there.
- The speaker's own income claim and the community income anecdotes are self-stated, not independently verified in the transcript.
