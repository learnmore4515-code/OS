## What this video is actually about
Liam Ottley (founder of Morningside Ai) teaches beginners how to price AI products and services when starting an AI automation agency. He covers why pricing matters, the value formula (skills + experience), the difference between product and service pricing, how to handle your first projects (free or at cost), and his recommended beginner pricing method: Cost Plus pricing with a starting 2x markup and 50% upfront / 50% on completion payment terms for projects under $20,000–$30,000. A slideshow resource is offered free via his school Community. The video explicitly notes it only scratches the surface (retainers vs upfronts, milestone pricing, value-based pricing, price-maxing data collection are excluded).

## Key lessons
- Pricing is the foundation of your agency: price must exceed delivery cost to make a profit, otherwise you don't have a business.
- Price is a proxy for value: people assume more expensive things are more valuable.
- Pricing shapes how your business operates (one big project every few months vs many small ones; retainers).
- Effective pricing generates enough profit to reinvest in marketing, product, and team.
- Value = skills + experience. Skills are technical abilities (e.g., building chatbots on Voiceflow, automating with make.com). Experience is practical wisdom from real deliveries; it reduces project risk, speeds implementation, and aligns solutions with business needs.
- Services are typically charged by time; products by perceived/real value (needs data to prove).
- Beginners lack data/proof of results, so start with AI development services rather than products.
- Self-deliver your first projects using no-code/low-code so you have no cost and can work for free to build experience.
- If you must delegate to a developer, charge at cost (not free) to cover costs.
- Prioritize learning before earning; don't rush to money or skip the learning phase.
- After a few free/low-cost projects, use Cost Plus pricing: estimate hours, apply a developer hourly rate, add markup (start 2x, scale to 3x–7x).
- Use 50% upfront / 50% on completion terms for projects under ~$20,000–$30,000 so the upfront payment covers your costs.
- Experience is more valuable than money when starting out; it's the only way to succeed long term.

## Sales
Fully scope the project, determine what the client needs, estimate time and resources required and the cost, get the developer hourly rate ($30–$50/hour depending on skill/experience), apply markup, and present a proposal/quote based on hours. Communicate clearly, show proof (past builds, personal builds, testimonials) to establish reasonable likelihood of delivery. Define completion/deliverable clearly in the contract to avoid disputes on final payment. Payment terms: 50% upfront / 50% on completion for projects under ~$20,000–$30,000; the upfront 50% covers expected costs (especially with a 2x markup).

## Fulfillment
Self-deliver using no-code/low-code tools (Voiceflow for chatbots, make.com for automation) so there's no cost, or delegate to a hired developer ($30–$50/hour). Start with straightforward, basic projects matching your skill level; developers can jump higher to minimize cost/complexity and accelerate learning before tackling complex projects. Deliver projects through to completion to gain experience working with clients and collect data.

## Exact tactics
- Offer first projects free (self-delivered, no-code/low-cost) to build experience and case studies.
- If hiring a developer, charge clients at cost for the first client/couple of clients; explain you value their testimonial more than the money.
- Use Cost Plus pricing after initial successes: scope fully, estimate hours × developer rate ($30–50/hr) × markup (start 2x).
- Structure payment 50% upfront / 50% on completion for projects under ~$20k–$30k; define "completion" in the contract.
- Post in a free community (his school Community) to get your first client quickly.
- Collect testimonials and before/after data from early deliveries to prove value and raise prices later.
- Start with basic no-code/low-code projects matching your skill level; increase rates as experience grows.
- Prioritize learning before earning; take less money upfront to stay in the game long term.

## Mistakes & warnings
- Charging too much too early / "shooting too high for your current skill level" → failure after failure.
- Rushing to money and skipping the learning phase → "chewed up and spat out" by the AI space.
- Charging clients while learning on their business ("pay me to learn") — described as "a bit Rich"; better to work free or at cost first.
- Beginners selling big expensive packages with no baby steps or deliveries (speaker recounts a community Q&A example).
- Pocketing a few thousand dollars in markup as a beginner increases client anger if delivery falls short; at-cost pricing makes clients more lenient.
- Not defining completion/deliverable in the contract → getting "pissed around" on final payment.
- Making up value numbers without data ("taking a stab in the dark").
- Going straight to products/value-based pricing before having proof of results.
- Speaker notes every "Ed OT right now thinks they're an AI expert just because they play around with chat GPT and Claude" with no delivery experience.
