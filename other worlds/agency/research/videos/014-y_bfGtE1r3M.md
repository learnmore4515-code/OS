## What this video is actually about
Liam Ottley breaks down "the simplest AI offer" a complete beginner can make to businesses to get a foot in the door of AI automation — no development team, no development experience, no experience getting on calls, and no lead-generation skill required. The offer is AI Tools Consulting: helping business owners identify the best AI tools to use in their existing processes and for their team, without doing any implementation or building yourself. It is positioned as the smallest "baby step" first offer toward becoming a successful AI automation agency owner. Intended audience: beginners who don't know what to offer, how to do market research, how to generate leads, or what niche to pick.

## Key lessons
- Money is made from the knowledge gap: the business owner knows little about AI (~10 on a 0-100 knowledge scale), and anyone who knows more (~25-65) can sell into that gap; you do not need to be the best in the world (100), just know more than the client.
- The easiest way to start is selling other people's stuff — existing AI tools that already have defined marketing and good products — rather than building or implementing everything yourself.
- You can start at ~25 knowledge versus the client's ~10 and still profit from the gap.
- Delivery is a three-step process: free consult → match tools from an internal database (plus industry-specific deep dive) → written report walked through on a call.
- Monetize before you can charge for consulting: affiliate commissions when clients subscribe to recommended tools, and referral fees by passing implementation-ready clients to implementation partners.
- Do not start if you need money immediately (e.g., bills next week); expect to do free consults for 1-2 months while building skill, confidence, and happy clients.
- The test of quality is client happiness: get feedback after the call and again a week or two after implementation; you want them saying it was "massively helpful."
- The consult moves clients from unaware/unfamiliar with AI to receptive to implementation — which is the prerequisite for later selling $20k-$40k AI projects.

## Exact business model discussed
AI Tools Consulting as a lead-generation / referral model, not done-for-you implementation:
1. Build an internal database of AI tools (researched via YouTube "best tools" videos, hands-on play, tutorials).
2. Offer a free AI tools consult / 30-minute AI tools audit to businesses.
3. On the call, ask many questions about the business and departments, take notes (no pressure to recommend on the call itself).
4. Match tools from your internal database, then do a further deep dive specific to the business/industry using directories ("There's an AI for That", Future Tools).
5. Deliver a written report (Canva template or plain doc, branding optional) and walk the client through it on a follow-up call or send it directly.
6. Get compensated via (a) affiliate commissions when the business subscribes to recommended tools, and (b) referral fees (10-20%) by passing clients ready for implementation to implementation partners (e.g., Morningside's Partners program).
Over time, expand into charging directly for consulting, then implementation.

## Offer
AI Tools Consulting: identify the best AI tools for a business's existing processes and team. Delivered as a free (initially) 30-minute AI tools audit/consult followed by a written tools report. No implementation, no development team, no building yourself — you sell/recommend other people's tools.

## Sales
- Run free consults initially (1-2 months, depending on volume) to build skill, confidence, and happy clients; do not pressure yourself to deliver recommendations on the call.
- Follow up by email a week or two after implementation; the key question is whether the client is happy / says it was "massively helpful."
- Once you have a good lead-generation flow, start charging: Liam suggests ~$100 as a starting paid price. His own consulting calls went $150-200 → $300 → $500 → $1,000 ($999/$997) as demand grew, and people still paid at $1,000.
- Do not sell implementation ($20k-$40k projects) until the client understands the tech and sees it as the best play for them right now.

## Fulfillment
Three-step process:
1. Free consult: ask a whole bunch of questions about the business and different departments, take notes; don't try to shoot off tool recommendations immediately if you're just starting out.
2. Match + find: match clients to tools from your internal database, then do a further deep dive for their business and industry specifically.
3. Report: write up a report (Canva template, Google Docs / "nice doc", blank document fine initially) and walk them through it on another call or send it directly, getting feedback.
Tools-database build method: watch YouTube "I tried a bajillion AI tools" / best-tools videos; list links and names in Google Sheet or Notion with categorization (creative, AI image generator, video generator, writing tools); narrow to a short list of the best per category; deep dive each via YouTube ("[tool] AI tutorial", "best X AI use cases") and Google Search for obscure tools; play with the tool; build a database of ~50 tools with write-ups, rankings, and tier/use-case filters (Notion recommended for filtering).

## Pricing
- Consult/audit: free initially (lead magnet); can continue free as a lead magnet.
- Suggested paid starting point: ~$100 per consult once you have traffic/leads coming in.
- Liam's personal consulting price path: started $150-200 → jacked to $300 → $500 → $1,000 ($999/$997) as call volume grew.
- Monetization (not client fees): affiliate commissions of 15-20% of the monthly recurring that companies pay for recommended tools; partner referral fees of 10-20% of deal size (example deal 5-10K initially), yielding "a few hundred maybe like $200" up to "maybe $1,000 per month."
- Liam's cited results: $4,300 within his 2nd or 3rd month of YouTube + consulting; "probably ... $220,000 off $999/$997 calls" (transcript garbled: "probably $15 $220,000").
- Affiliate income churn: clients may use a tool for 2 months and fall off; pick good tools and longer-term, decent-sized businesses.

## Exact tactics
- Put tool links/names plus categorization in a sheet or Notion; filter by tier and use case.
- On calls, when a client mentions a need (e.g., an SEO writing team), pull matching tools from your database into recommendations.
- Deep-dive each tool: YouTube search "[tool] AI tutorial" / "best X AI use cases"; Google Search for obscure tools.
- Report: Canva template or plain document; walk through on a second call to get feedback.
- Warm-outreach framing: "I've built this entire database of AI tools... I'm helping businesses with free 30-minute AI tools audits — do you know anyone who'd be interested?" (ask for referrals, not a direct sale).
- Video CTAs: two per video (early and end): free AI tools consult; book via Calendly link below.
- Content angles: industry-by-industry best AI tools (examples: builders, plumbers).
- Track happiness/feedback after calls and 1-2 weeks post-implementation.

## Mistakes & warnings
- Don't start this if you need to pay bills next week — it takes significant time to get running; "maybe one in a 100" hustles their way to money.
- Don't jump straight to implementation/dev services; that takes months of learning, tutorials, and practice.
- Don't try to deliver recommendations on the free consult call if you're not good enough yet — take notes and follow up.
- Don't let fancy branding/templates hold you back — a blank document is fine initially.
- Don't use Facebook ads as a beginner to get clients.
- Don't cold-email as a beginner unless you're familiar with it and have tested copy/targeting.
- Never sell $20k-$40k AI projects to someone who doesn't first understand the tech and why it's good for their business.
- Expect churn in affiliate income (clients use a tool 2 months and fall off); match tools carefully and target longer-term users.
- Don't make the content/outreach a direct sales pitch for his paid community/accelerator (he explicitly says he doesn't want it to be a sales pitch).
