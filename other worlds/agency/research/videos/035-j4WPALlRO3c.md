## What this video is actually about
Michael Tabirade lays out how he would become an AI consultant in 2026, based on a room he was in with non-executive directors, trustees, and CEOs, plus conferences and events he attended the previous week. His core thesis: business leaders are not asking which AI tool to use (they already know); they are asking what happens to their clients' data when it goes into tools like ChatGPT. The consultant opportunity he describes is in AI governance, information security, data privacy, and strategy — helping organizations implement AI safely and securely — rather than purely building tools. He walks through five deployment options for clients, then gives three starter actions for consultants. He also promotes his own 6-week sprints program on becoming a successful independent consultant and a newsletter sign-up.

## Key lessons
- CEOs/trustees' real question is data confidentiality ("what happens to our clients' data when it goes into ChatGPT?"), not tool selection. The speaker says if you don't know the answer, you shouldn't be using the tool.
- Speaker-cited MIT study (2025, 300 businesses): only 5% got a return on investment from AI; beneficial use cases were admin and a bit of sales.
- Speaker-cited "Iceberg Index": visible tech changes (e.g., Klarna) are the tip of the iceberg; beneath it are unmeasured HR/admin/ops skills with potential to be turned into AI workflows and efficiencies.
- Speaker says ~90% of employees use personal AI tools (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude) on the side for emails, technical info, and research, but hedges: "I don't know if it's 90%"; most have no green light from their leader/organization.
- The problem is not the tools themselves; it is understanding the governance, architecture, and strategy required to use them appropriately.
- AI exposes challenges a business already has; if systems/structure are weak, AI makes that harder. The AI conversation is also about structuring the business well — strategy, delivery, risk, and governance are key.
- Every organization needs an AI policy that informs behavior. At an event with trustees, 90-95% of the room did not have AI as a board item.
- The consultant's job is to match the right deployment option to the organization's risk profile (compliance, law, government, perception, target audience) and budget.
- You don't need to build it — just understand it well enough to advise (review/audit, strategy; may not even discuss AI initially, could be strategy or another delivery).
- Roles to choose from: advisor, lead, person delivering it, bring someone in, or just the audit.
- UK/Europe is not Silicon Valley: different procurement rules, trustee understandings, behaviors, culture, and legal exposure — especially for UK public sector, charities, and professional services, which need consultants who understand information governance, data privacy, and security.
- You don't need to be a data architect, but you must ask the right questions and be absorbed in the space (events, videos, books, white papers, reports).
- Starter actions for consultants: (1) read one AI policy or notice an organization without one; (2) learn the five deployment types and explain the architecture to a non-technical CEO; (3) research RAG.

## Fulfillment
Five deployment options presented for clients:
1. Commercial tools + AI policy: ChatGPT (GPT 5.5 mentioned), Opus 4.7, Gemini (3.1/4 mentioned), Microsoft Copilot, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure — with privacy/protection documentation aligned to the organization.
2. Local models: Ollama (ChatGPT-like GUI) running open-source LLMs locally (Google's Gemma 4, Qwen, DeepSeek) — no internet required, device-only; e.g., pre-installed Ollama + Gemma 4.
3. APIs: MCPs and webhooks as connectors so programs/software/CRMs talk to each other (e.g., main chat interface connected to different tools); APIs can be built manually or with AI-generated guidance; consultant can check providers' agreements or negotiate bespoke agreements for the client.
4. Private cloud server: Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, AWS (Amazon's AWS), running open-source models as the AI layer for a client tool (Qwen/DeepSeek noted as Chinese-based APIs; Gemini 4 as Google-based).
5. Fine-tuning or RAG (e.g., LangChain; Notebook LM — up to 300 documents/bits of data processed; Google Workspace projects). Developing your own LLM is described as very expensive, slow, and investment-heavy — even ChatGPT has been losing money.
Process: document SOPs for one department first (not all at once); map process flow, required integrations, pipeline, and tools to connect; weigh cost, scalability, cloud vs local, data quality, code quality, security/privacy, and governance. Match option to risk profile and budget; the conversation may start with strategy rather than AI.

## Exact tactics
- Document SOPs for one department first, not every department at once.
- Map process flow, required integrations, the pipeline, and the tools to connect/list.
- Evaluate cost, scalability, cloud vs local, data quality, code quality, security/privacy, and governance before recommending an option.
- Read one AI policy to understand it, or identify an organization that lacks one.
- Learn the five deployment types and be able to explain their architecture to a non-technical CEO.
- Research RAG; understand why organizations want AI trained on their own data/documents/context.
- Help a client develop an AI policy if missing, or review and question their existing one.
- Attend events and read white papers/reports to absorb the space.
- As consultant, pick a role (advisor/lead/deliverer/brings someone in/audit only) and match the option to the client's risk profile and budget.

## Mistakes / warnings
- Putting client data into ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini without knowing where it goes — likely a breach of GDPR/Data Protection promises; "shouldn't be using it" if you don't know.
- FOMO-driven AI adoption (fear of missing out / fear of being slower).
- Iterating AI responses nine times out of ten when writing from scratch would have been faster.
- Building your own LLM: expensive, slow, investment-heavy — even ChatGPT loses money; unrealistic for smaller companies (as spoken).
- Copying the US YouTube "it's easy to set up an AI agency with Claude Code / MCP" model without adapting to UK/Europe procurement, trustee, culture, and legal differences.
- Not needing to be a data architect is not an excuse to skip asking the right governance questions.
