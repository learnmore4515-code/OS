## What this video is actually about
A step-by-step walkthrough of becoming an AI consultant (which the guest calls "Vibe consulting": professional services that are AI native, using AI for both acquisition and delivery) and landing your first client without needing an audience. Host Eliot Prince sits down with Andrew Dunn, who walks through the opportunity, the three service models, a diagnosis-first consulting method, pricing/revenue models, and how to get first clients. It is not a build-along tutorial; it is a business-model and sales-process walkthrough grounded in the guest's consulting practice and client anecdotes.

## Key lessons
- Vibe/AI consulting = professional services that are AI native: use AI for acquisition and for delivery, giving "ultimate leverage". Non-technical people can enter — the guest says he struggles to turn on his computer and yet consults for massive companies.
- Consulting is white-collar knowledge work; AI makes it more efficient than ever. The consulting market (~$1.06 trillion per the guest) is larger than SaaS ($370B), digital creative agencies ($276B), and digital marketing agencies ($750B) per the guest's figures; the AI automation agency market is ~$22B by his figure.
- The consultant sells clarity, not tools: the client walks away with a clear roadmap of how to AI-transform. Creative agencies sell output/assets; AI automation sells a system/workflow.
- "Prescription without diagnosis is malpractice": diagnose the root problem before prescribing any AI solution; most AI sellers walk in with a solution (chatbot/workflow) without hunting for the problem.
- Two missing pieces in most AI implementations: strategy (diagnosis + roadmap) and change management/adoption (upskilling staff so they actually use it). The guest cites an MIT study: 95% of AI implementations failed to deliver meaningful ROI. The top 20% with strategy + roadmap + implementation + adoption see 100%+ ROIs.
- Engagement mechanics: find expensive problems, quantify the cost (labor wastage, tool wastage), prescribe the solution, tie everything to a monetary amount. If you can't tie back money or time, you can't measure ROI, and you don't get paid.
- Current state / target state / measurable delta between them is the core of the discovery phase.
- Four questions to run every task through: input structured? decisions rule-based? output predictable? repeated often?
- Prioritize with an opportunities matrix: quick wins = highest value, least cost/difficulty (the "springboard" that finances big swings); big swings = high value but take longer.
- Payback benchmark: 12-month payback = home run for big companies; the guest's implementations are always under 12 months payback.
- Revenue ladder: audit/assessment → implementation → maintenance → advisory; can expand into productized platform/SaaS or training.
- First clients come from your warm network (phone, email, LinkedIn — "Rolodex"); networking events also work. No audience required.
- Undercharging is endemic ("people undercharge like crazy"); price on value/ROI, not on ease of delivery.

## Exact business model discussed
AI/Vibe consulting: a professional-services business that diagnoses where AI can transform a client's business, prescribes a roadmap, and charges for the diagnosis (audit/assessment), then optionally implementation, maintenance, and advisory. The consultant is the "key to the AI door" — fractional AI advisor — rather than a software builder. Delivery can be done by the consultant, partnered/white-labeled to implementers, or handed off. Four expansion paths are described: (1) pure consulting/strategic advisory (roadmap to AI transform), (2) full-stack consulting (strategy + implementation + advisory + maintenance), (3) productize recurring problems into a platform/SaaS, (4) training/workforce upskilling. All paths start with consulting.

## Customer
Businesses with expensive, measurable problems — primarily white-collar/knowledge-work companies. Examples given: SDR sales teams (8 reps), property management firms (short-term rental invoicing), investment firms (deceased-property lead sourcing), manufacturing/engineering companies ($30M/year, 50+ people, owner-reliant, 60–80-year-old owners doing quoting/estimation), accounting practices (UK, using Xero), IPO companies (quarterly/earnings reports), solar companies (500+ door-to-door reps), trucking/transportation companies. Small 5–10 person businesses just want to grow; bigger companies buy outcomes/ROI. Blue-collar services (e.g., a lone plumber with no competition) are noted as the exception that may not need AI. The guest's community includes non-technical people who "barely got a ChatGPT account" as potential consultants.

## Problem
Businesses have inefficiencies they can't quantify; staff waste time on repetitive manual work (e.g., SDRs spending 2 hours/day before their first call); owners are bottlenecks/key-man risks (80-year-old owner still doing all quotes); AI tools get bought off-the-shelf (Fiverr/Upwork workflows) without diagnosis, change management, or adoption, so 3 months later the tool collects dust — 95% of AI implementations fail to deliver meaningful ROI per the cited MIT study. Founders are too far from day-to-day operations to know the real bottlenecks ("executives know the destination, operators know the road").

## Offer
Diagnosis-first AI consulting: an audit/assessment that finds expensive problems, quantifies their cost, and delivers a roadmap deck (current state, target state, measurable delta, workflows identified, AI solutions, costs, roadmap). Then implementation, maintenance (MSP-style recurring upkeep as AI evolves and things break), and advisory (fractional AI person, $3–5k/month per the transcript's "three 5,000 a month", keeping the client on pulse of relevant AI changes). The offer sells outcomes/ROI (profit/EBITDA delta), not growth or features.

## Acquisition
Warm network first: phone contacts, email, LinkedIn — someone in your Rolodex is likely open to it. First deals reported to close in a day, 3 days, or a week because they come from warm networking. If no warm network, start prospecting; networking events work (two community members closed paid deals at their first networking event). Entry options for the first client: do it free to build skill/confidence/case studies, or a back-end deal (no money up front; "a couple of thousand" if satisfied, in exchange for case studies and references). Andrew Dunn says he advises against free assessments, though two community members (Alex, Derek) gave away free/cheap assessments and still landed large contracts. Content/YouTube presence is mentioned as the guest's own channel but he explicitly says you don't need the AI knowledge or audience from his channel to do this.

## Sales
Human-to-human selling; diagnosis de-risks the solution for the buyer. Big businesses pay for diagnosis. Sales framing: show current profit (e.g., $10M ARR, $1.5M net profit) and the delta to improved profit ($2.2M), not revenue growth. Use the annual-waste formula: time wasted × number of people × days per year × loaded hourly cost = annual waste, then price a fraction of that (guest floated $60,000 for the $166,000/year SDR waste; notes others undercharge at $2k–$5k). Standard advice: 12-month payback is a home run; big companies evaluate on 10-year horizons. Objection handling: clients often say they want the whole thing automated so they can sit on the beach — the guest pushes back that this is "la-la land". Paid-from-day-one is recommended; even $500 from a first paying client proves the process.

## Fulfillment
Discovery: ask deep questions to executives and operators; "walk me through exactly what you did yesterday morning" and keep asking "and what did you do then? / why?" to surface every click and inefficiency. Map end-to-end processes (most businesses have never mapped them). Identify bottlenecks AI can solve. Prioritize via opportunities matrix (quick wins first, then big swings). Deliver: roadmap, then implementation (can be white-labeled/partnered to implementers — the guest took 10–15% off the top as a partner, later white-labeled with margins up to 50%), then maintenance as recurring revenue (MSP model; clients are locked in because maintaining the AI systems is hard to leave), plus advisory. Fulfillment does not require the consultant to be technical — implementers on Upwork etc. can be partnered with. Big custom platform implementations are noted as "insanely stressful"; the guest avoids them now.

## Exact tactics
- Open with diagnosis, never a solution: ask executives and operators where they're bleeding money, what they hate, what they haven't fixed; map processes end-to-end (9–5 walkthrough of every click).
- Quantify with current state → target state → measurable delta tied to money/time (e.g., lead response 34 hours → under 5 minutes; conversion 20% → 24%; 5 hours/week content → zero hours; out-of-hours coverage 0% → 100% weekends).
- Run every task through four questions: structured input? rule-based decisions? predictable output? repeated often?
- Prioritize via opportunities matrix: quick wins first (springboard that finances big swings), then big swings.
- Price using annual-waste formula; aim for under-12-month payback; quote a fraction of the waste, not a cost-plus build price.
- Deliverable: a presentation deck with where they are now, identified workflows, AI solutions, costs, and roadmap.
- First client: warm network (phone/email/LinkedIn); if none, prospect and attend networking events; offer free or back-end deal initially to get case studies; move to paid quickly (even $500).
- Non-technical consultants: partner with or white-label implementers (Upwork etc.); avoid building big custom platforms yourself.
- Recurring revenue: maintenance (MSP model — keep delivered solutions working as AI evolves) and advisory ($3–5k/month fractional AI person).
- Expansion: productize recurring problems → platform/SaaS; or training/upskilling workforce.
- Outreach script: "I show businesses how they can AI transform and become AI native, not AI absent."

## Mistakes / warnings
- Selling AI chatbots/workflows without diagnosis ("prescription without diagnosis is malpractice"); the guest calls chatbot-slingers his "villain".
- Bolting AI onto a broken operation ("Lamborghini bumpers on a Toyota Corolla") — no strategy, no roadmap.
- No change management/adoption: tools bought on Fiverr/Upwork collect dust within 3 months; staff weren't bought in.
- Undercharging ("people undercharge like crazy"); pricing by ease of delivery rather than value.
- Building a SaaS before validating via consulting ("fatal mistake of building before they validated").
- Trying to automate the whole business so the owner can sit on the beach — "la-la land"; passive income is mostly a myth unless you have Warren Buffett-level capital.
- Technical implementers overwhelming prospects with workflows instead of outcomes — trainable, but a real failure mode.
- Free/cheap assessments: two community members made them work, but the guest says he always advises against giving them away.
- Don't force staff to learn AI if they won't; make the system easy to operate within instead ("don't force a square peg into a round hole").
- Don't add five AI tools to a team already juggling seven disconnected tools; first get one central source of truth/data.
- Big custom platform implementations are "insanely stressful" — the guest personally avoids them now.
