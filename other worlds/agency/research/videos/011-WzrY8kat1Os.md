## What this video is actually about
A beginner-to-pro roadmap for becoming an AI consultant. The speaker (Kia Ghasem, who says he has worked as an AI consultant for the past couple of years) argues that AI consulting is the future because AI development will become commoditized as coding agents improve, making strategy more valuable than development. He lays out a path: start as an AI agency owner doing one-off custom projects for businesses, gain skills, testimonials, and social proof, then transition into an AI transformation partner targeting mid-market businesses (50-250 employees, $5-10M/year revenue) with long-term transformation engagements built one project at a time. Ends with a pitch for his 28-day accelerator program.

## Key lessons
- AI consulting is the future; implementing AI into a business is a two-step process: (1) strategize — identify the best AI implementation opportunities with positive ROI; (2) develop custom solutions for those opportunities.
- As AI capabilities improve, development barriers keep decreasing; development will likely be commoditized, so strategy becomes way more important than development.
- Two business models compared: AI agency (one-off projects, development-heavy, money from development, consulting as gateway to projects) vs AI transformation partner (long-term partnership, full road map, more valued as consultant than developer, higher LTV, easier repeat sales).
- Target mid-market businesses: 50-250 employees, likely making at least $5-10 million/year — they have money but can't afford big consulting firms like McKinsey.
- Six-step AI transformation partner process: discovery → diagnose problems/bottlenecks → build project road map → develop projects → train staff → transform entire business from manual to AI augmented.
- Roadmap for a beginner: start as AI agency/freelancer doing one-off projects → gain testimonials/social proof → add consulting/discovery + road maps → train staff (even free practice) → foot-in-the-door (small project → bigger projects) → pitch consulting/transformation retainers → after multiple projects + case study, position as AI transformation partner targeting mid-market.
- LTV increases because repeat projects for the same client mean easier sales, less discovery, and more trust each time.
- Do NOT try to be an AI transformation partner right off the bat as a complete beginner — too difficult (lack of experience, hard to pitch, business takes a big leap of faith). Start as an AI agency first to gain skills and experience.

## Exact business model discussed
AI transformation partner model, reached via an AI agency first phase. The agency phase: freelancer doing custom one-off projects for businesses (development-heavy, money from development, consulting as gateway). The transformation partner phase: partner with a business to completely transform its processes with AI — build a full road map, automate processes one by one using AI and automation, train staff, charge via retainer or per-project basis, maximize customer lifetime value through repeated sequential projects. Ends with an accelerator program offer (28 days to become an AI expert and set up an agency).

## Customer

- Initial customers (agency phase): businesses generally — any business that comes with requirements for a custom one-off project (example given: an accounting firm).

## Problem

- Big consulting firms charge too much for mid-market businesses to afford AI transformation.

- Development is becoming commoditized; strategy (knowing what to build and in what sequence) is the scarce, valuable part.
- Businesses need to trust a consultant before agreeing to a multi-month/multi-year transformation — hard for beginners to establish that trust.

## Exact tactics
- Foot-in-the-door technique: build one small project, gain trust, then pitch bigger projects.
- Full discovery sessions to understand the client's business back to front.
- Diagnose bottlenecks/waste and quantify ROI before proposing automation.

- Train client staff (Perplexity/ChatGPT, prompt engineering) — free practice early to build confidence and trust.
- Pitch consulting/transformation retainers after the first project proves ROI.
- Change marketing and ICP to mid-market only after a case study exists.
- Deliver via retainer or per-project basis; consider internal team for development to maximize LTV.

- Don't pitch transformation without a case study to show for it.
- Don't target big enterprises (they go to McKinsey) or tiny businesses (no money) — aim mid-market once proven.
