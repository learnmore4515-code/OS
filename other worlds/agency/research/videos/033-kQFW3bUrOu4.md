## What this video is actually about
Liam Ottley (Morningside AI) open-sourcing his agency's three-phase playbook for taking any business from "organized chaos" to order to growth using AI: Phase 1 education/alignment (leadership workshops), Phase 2 identification (audit → AI strategy roadmap), Phase 3 development (build AI systems, starting with quick wins). Two audiences: business owners who want to be in the 5% seeing AI ROI, and entrepreneurs/agency owners who want to serve the 95% failing with AI. Also a career path for agency owners: no-code AI automation agency → AI agency with a hired full-stack AI developer → AI transformation partner (AITP) selling the full transformation playbook. A free AI audit resource (questionnaires, key slides, ROI calcs) is offered in the description; Morningside takes on partners by application only.

## Key lessons
- Start with process, not technology: the 5% getting massive ROI start with a process; most businesses dive straight into tooling and fail.
- 95% of AI initiatives fail to deliver ROI (MIT study, enterprise context); most businesses get zero value from AI.
- Companies are "organized chaos": scattered data, "how we've always done it" processes, dozens of non-integrated apps, plus shadow AI (employees secretly using AI tools → data-leak risk). Plugging powerful AI into that chaos fails miserably.
- Align the leadership team first (2-hour AI leadership workshop + employee workshop) before presenting recommendations — bought-in leadership means no surprises and budget.
- Become the world's leading expert on how the client's company actually works: deep-dive interviews from department heads to frontline staff, with personalized, evolving interview guides.
- Map core workflows visually (Figma); most clients have never seen an objective map of how their business operates day to day.
- Identify use cases by overlaying proven AI solutions (Morningside's internal 300+ system database) onto process-map bottlenecks — manual data entry, report writing, repetitive tasks.
- Prioritize with an opportunity matrix: quick wins (high impact, low difficulty) plus big swings; never build in isolation — validate with both employees (tools won't get used without their buy-in) and leadership (no budget without their buy-in).
- Deliverable is a 50–100 page AI strategy roadmap report.
- Always start development with quick wins, never a six-figure six-month moonshot first ("a surefire way to lose trust and momentum").
- Biggest quick wins are in "boring" places: manual data entry, document creation, constant lookups — voice agents, smart transcription, internal document QA agents; can save teams hundreds of hours a month and hundreds of thousands of dollars.
- Quick wins cost anywhere from $20,000 to $50,000 or more.
- Development needs talented full-stack AI developers; find them in skilled communities; keep delivery lean (devs handle client communication).
- Long-term partnerships drive LTV: some Morningside clients over a year, approaching two; offer upgrades when new models drop (e.g., GPT-5).
- Land one or two major transformation projects per quarter and stack/layer them for a predictable, high-margin business.
- It took Morningside years to reach the AITM model — about two years in the trenches before selling the full transformation playbook.

## Exact tactics
- Run the leadership workshop before presenting any recommendations; get decision makers to understand key terms and what's at stake.
- Use the AI first/org chart slide to show the end-state vision.
- Write personalized interview guides per person; adapt questions as you learn more.
- Interview everyone from department heads to frontline staff.
- Map core workflows visually in Figma from interview data.
- Overlay a proven-solutions database onto process-map bottlenecks (manual data entry, report writing, repetitive tasks).
- Score opportunities on an opportunity matrix; eliminate to a mix of quick wins and big swings.
- Run validation interviews with employees and leadership before finalizing (both buy-ins required).
- Deliver a 50–100 page AI strategy roadmap.
- Build quick wins first (high impact, low difficulty); never lead with a six-month moonshot.
- Quote cost-estimate ranges with breathing room for unknowns.
- Hire one versatile full-stack AI developer (ideally full-stack) from skilled communities; let developers handle client communication.
- Offer existing clients upgrades when new models drop (e.g., GPT-5).
- Target 1–2 major transformation projects per quarter; stack and layer them.
- Business-owner DIY option: run an audit one department at a time, find one pilot project, post in developer communities for help.

## Mistakes / warnings
- Starting with the technology/tool instead of the process → failure.
- Diving straight into development after getting hyped by a new tool → ultimate failure.
- Copying what neighbors/headlines/YouTube show instead of doing groundwork exploratory audits.
- Using AI for things it isn't good at yet (chasing experimental over production-proven use cases).
- Leading development with a six-month, multi-million-dollar moonshot → "a surefire way to lose trust and momentum."
- Designing solutions in isolation without employee validation → tools won't get used.
- Missing leadership buy-in → no budget.
- Expecting it to be easy: "not going to sugar coat it... make a lot of mistakes."
- Morningside is selective with partners; not every business qualifies.
