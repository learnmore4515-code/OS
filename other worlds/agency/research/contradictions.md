# Contradictions

Conflicting advice preserved across creators, with derived conditional rules. Sources are video row numbers from `video-research-ledger.md`.

## Format per contradiction
- The conflict: Creator A says X; Creator B says Y.
- Who succeeded with each, at what stage, with what acquisition channel, service type, market, timeframe, skill level.
- Derived conditional rule.

## 1. Niche-first vs. broad-first positioning

- **Creator A (niche-first):** Nicole McCain (063) — niche-focused consulting; Liam Ottley (015) — niche via 4-factor unfair-advantage audit (business access > experience/connections > interest).
- **Creator B (broad-first / broaden later):** Liam Ottley (018 — "5 ways to start an AI agency," can broaden later); Kia Ghasem (011 — broaden after you have a case study); Carson Reed (002 — picked real estate but acquired via paid ads + outbound).
- **Who succeeded with each:** Liam Ottley's own revenue came via YouTube/personal brand (broad inbound), yet his 015 framework advises niche-first via unfair advantage — he used both at different stages. Carson Reed succeeded with a niche (real estate) but via paid outbound, not niche-driven inbound.
- **Derived conditional rule:** Niche-first is strongest when acquisition depends on targeted outbound or a specific productized offer. Broader positioning works when the founder already has inbound demand or a personal brand. For a $0 founder with no brand, start with the niche you already have access to (your own/friend's business), then broaden after the first case study.

## 2. Cold outreach vs. warm network / inbound first

- **Creator A (cold outreach):** Carson Reed (002 — Meta ads + outbound, closed 2 clients ~day 5–7); Andrew Dunn (024 — cold email via Clay/instantly/heyreach/Apollo, claimed 153 clients from 14,100 emails at 8.7%); Albert Olgaard (028 — Google Maps call/text 20 businesses/day).
- **Creator B (warm network / inbound first):** Nate Herk (004 — warm email/social/phone contacts; 008 — inbound via free value/audits; 061 — 20-person trust map → free pilot → paid maintenance/expansion); Liam Ottley (015 — connection/referral-led).
- **Who succeeded with each:** Carson Reed's success used paid ads to amplify outreach (not pure $0 cold outreach). Andrew Dunn used volume + tooling (Clay, Instantly). Nate Herk and Liam Ottley's 015 both succeeded with warm/referral-led first clients. Albert Olgaard warns most cold-outreach practitioners quit early (he took 4 months; "7 days" is aspirational).
- **Derived conditional rule:** Cold outreach is viable only with high volume, a specific offer + niche, and 4+ months of runway — it is NOT a reliable week-1 channel. For a true $0 start with no brand, warm network/referral-first (trust map → discovery → free pilot → paid) is the lower-risk path; defer cold outreach until after several proof cycles.

## 3. Cost-plus / time-based pricing vs. value / ROI-based pricing

- **Creator A (cost-plus / time-based):** Liam Ottley (017 — beginner Cost Plus = dev rate $30–50/hr × estimated hours × markup, start 2x → 3x–7x; 50% upfront / 50% completion under $20K–$30K).
- **Creator B (value / ROI-based):** Nate Herk (008 — anchor off value added; client should see 10x ROI within a year; B2B $3K–$10K project closable in a week); Nick Puru (036 — ROI model built before building; price by what the problem costs).
- **Who succeeded with each:** Liam Ottley's method is aimed at beginners with no data. Nate Herk's value-based pricing applies to established B2B consulting ($3K–$10K projects). Nick Puru's ROI model applies to done-for-you transformation ($81K case study).
- **Derived conditional rule:** Use cost-plus / time-based pricing when you have no client data or proof (beginner stage). Shift to value / ROI-based pricing once you have client results and can quantify the problem's cost. Price must exceed delivery cost either way, or there is no business.

## 4. First client free/discounted vs. paid from day one

- **Creator A (free/discounted first client):** Liam Ottley (007 — $1,000 case-study rate vs $2,000–$3,000 typical; discounted to secure first real client + proof; showed Stripe dashboard proof); Liam Ottley (017 — first projects free if self-delivered, or at cost if delegating); Nick Saraev (026 — free to build case studies, or back-end deal).
- **Creator B (paid from day one):** Eliot Prince (025 — "$500 is fine for a first paid client"; paid-from-day-one recommended).
- **Who succeeded with each:** Liam Ottley's first client was real and paid (even if discounted), with Stripe proof shown. Eliot Prince's advice is preference, not demonstrated outcome.
- **Derived conditional rule:** Free or at-cost is acceptable ONLY for the very first 1–2 clients to obtain proof/testimonials. Move to paid quickly; "$500 is fine for a first paid client" is a healthier default than working free indefinitely.

## 5. Sell "AI" / tools vs. sell outcomes / diagnosis

- **Creator A (sell "AI"):** Several creators market AI tools/automations as the product (implicit in most "AI agency" framing).
- **Creator B (sell outcomes):** Nate Herk (008 — "destination, not the vehicle"; prospects don't buy workflows, they buy diagnosis + prescription); Nick Puru (036 — the named problem is usually a symptom; the real problem is upstream).
- **Who succeeded with each:** Nate Herk's B2B consulting ($3K–$10K projects) and Nick Puru's done-for-you transformation ($81K case study) are outcome-led. Pure "sell AI" framing is more common among B2C info-product creators (weaker evidence).
- **Derived conditional rule:** Lead with the client's painful outcome (time, money, focus), not the technology. Do discovery first, map the business, tie one tool to each pain point, and present diagnosis + prescription on the sales call. "AI" is the delivery mechanism, not the offer.

## 6. Price points vary wildly ($3000 client vs. $60000 audit vs. $10k/month)

- **Creator A:** Liam Ottley (007 — $1,000 case-study chatbot; $2,000–$3,000 typical; $3,000–$8,000/mo retainer).
- **Creator B:** Nick Puru (036 — $81,000 implementation case study).
- **Creator C:** Eliot Prince (025 — audits $5K–$15K for sub-100-person businesses).
- **Creator D:** Carson Reed (002 — $5,000 upfront or $2,000/mo × 3 for 90-day appointment program).
- **Derived conditional rule:** Price depends on offer, niche, founder credibility, and client's problem cost — not a universal rate card. A chatbot lead-gen tool is a $1K–$3K project; a business-transformation audit/implementation is $5K–$81K+. Anchor price to the client's ROI (10x rule), not to your hours.

## 7. "$0 start is possible" vs. "need paid tools/ads"

- **Creator A ($0 start):** Liam Ottley (021 — "Best AI Business if You're BROKE," no-code/low-code self-delivery); Nate Herk (004 — warm outreach, no agency/team/brand/audience needed); Nick Puru (036 — self-deliver no-code so no cost).
- **Creator B (paid tools/ads implied):** Carson Reed (002 — used paid Meta ads + existing aged ad account + paid Go High Level account — not a clean $0 replication); Andrew Dunn (024 — paid cold-email tooling Clay/Instantly/HeyReach/Apollo).
- **Who succeeded with each:** Liam Ottley and Nick Puru describe starting with no-cost no-code tools. Carson Reed's "$0" claim is qualified — he had an existing paid GHL account and aged FB ad account. Andrew Dunn uses paid outreach tooling.
- **Derived conditional rule:** A true $0 start is possible using no-code/low-code self-delivery + warm network + free channels. Paid tools/ads accelerate but are not required; be skeptical of "$0" claims that quietly assume existing paid accounts/tooling.

## 8. "$10k/month in 90 days" vs. realistic timeline

- **Creator A ($10k/month in 90 days):** Albert Olgaard (028 — title hook); Automate AI Consulting (027, 034 — title hook).
- **Creator B (reality check):** Albert Olgaard himself (028 — "don't expect a client in the first 5 hours; most quit too early; he took 4 months"); Automate AI Consulting (034 — title hook only, no 90-day roadmap in transcript).
- **Derived conditional rule:** Treat "$10k/month in 90 days" as marketing framing, not a typical or guaranteed outcome. Realistic first-client timelines are weeks to months (Nate Herk's 7-day plan to first conversation; Carson Reed closed clients ~day 5–7 but with paid ads and prior experience).
