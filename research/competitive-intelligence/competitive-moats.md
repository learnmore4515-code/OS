# Competitive Moats — Analysis (Phase 2)

**Date:** 2026-10-27  
**Source:** Homepage observations across n8n, Zapier, Make, Vercel, LangChain, Thoughtworks (Phase 2 research).  
**Method:** Each candidate moat is tested against the question: "Can a smaller company reproduce the mechanism without reproducing the company's budget?"

## What the strongest companies consistently do that ordinary agencies do not

1. **Publish quantified, named proof.** Every reference company names customers and attaches metrics (hours saved, $ revenue, orders/day, tickets resolved). Ordinary agencies show logos at best, or generic "we work with insurance firms" claims. This is low-cost to produce (requires customer permission, not budget) and high-trust.

2. **Operate an education pillar (docs + academy + blog + newsletter).** Make Academy, LangChain Academy, n8n docs/community, LangChain blog+changelog. This creates repeat visits, SEO topical authority, and positions the company as a teacher, not just a vendor. Ordinary agencies publish nothing public.

3. **Offer a template / asset library.** n8n example workflows, Make Templates Library, Zapier Guided Templates. These are shareable, linkable, and convert platform-curious visitors into services leads. Ordinary agencies keep all IP in client engagements.

4. **Run a community or expert directory.** n8n (250k community, Discord, experts.n8n.io), Make (Community, Academy, "hire an expert"), LangChain (meetups, events, community). This creates network effects and a services referral channel. Ordinary agencies have no community mechanism.

5. **Provide low-friction first value.** Vercel (deploy without login), Make (free tier, no credit card), n8n (self-host or cloud free start). The visitor experiences value before talking to sales. Ordinary agencies demand a booking call as the first interaction.

6. **Speak to governance and trust, not just features.** Zapier (guardrails, SOC 2, CISO framing), n8n (human-in-the-loop, audit logs, "see + understand + trust"), LangChain (evals, observability, governance). This wins enterprise/insurance buyers who care about risk. Ordinary agencies lead with "we use AI."

## Moats LeoPisha should NOT chase yet

- **Analyst recognition / Gartner placement** (Thoughtworks): requires enterprise scale, analyst relationships, years. Not a near-term differentiator.
- **Massive integration ecosystem** (Zapier 9,000+ apps): platform-level play, not consultancy-level.
- **Custom community platform / member database:** the brief explicitly warns against building a custom forum without evidence of demand. Use existing platforms (LinkedIn, WhatsApp, email) first.
- **SaaS product / subscription:** the business must remain consultancy-first; subscriptions should not imitate software companies.

## Recommended moat sequence for LeoPisha

1. **Tier 1 (now):** Quantified named case studies (as clients permit) + governance/trust messaging + low-friction first value (one free assessment/calculator).
2. **Tier 2 (30–90 days):** Public n8n template library + education pillar (guides/blog/newsletter) + expert directory pilot.
3. **Tier 3 (later):** Community on an existing platform; only after there's an audience asking for it.
4. **Never (consultancy model):** Custom forum, member database, SaaS subscription, analyst-chasing.
